1.1 and 1.4 are the same bug, and it is diagnosable

The reports: "designerinit hardcodes DROP to 13:00 … overwriting your input of 60:00", "whenever I click apply it reverts the curve back to defaults when I try setting anything over 18, but it works for 17 minutes", and "clicking enter leads to restoring defaults".

Both halves are confirmed, and the 17-versus-18 detail is the clue that identifies the mechanism. From canvas.py:17935:

#CH, DE,    FCs,    FCe,     SCs,     SCe,       Drop,    COOL
self.designertimeinit = [0, 4*60, 9*60, 11*60, 12*60, 12.5*60, 13*60, 17*60]

DROP defaults to 13*60 = 13:00, exactly as reported — and COOL defaults to 17*60 = 17:00. Then settimes() (designer.py:350) runs two validations before applying anything, and returns early on either failure:

  • validatetime() — format check.
  • validatetimeorder() — times must be strictly ascending. Fail and it shows "Times need to be in ascending order" and returns, applying nothing.

So entering a DROP of 18:00 or more puts DROP after the COOL default of 17:00, the order check fails, and every field is left holding its previous value. 17:00 works because it still fits under COOL. Nothing is being "reset to defaults" — the edit is being silently refused, and the untouched defaults are what you are looking at. Individual fields have a second, quieter version of the same behaviour: each except branch does self.EditN.setText(self.EditNcopy), restoring the old text on any parse failure.

Fix: the defaults must scale to the roast being designed rather than being fixed constants, and a refused apply must say which pair is out of order rather than reverting silently. This is one change, not two, and it closes 1.1, 1.4 and most of 1.3.

The roast data in these notes should become the defaults

The notes carry real observations from the owner's roasting, and they are worth keeping somewhere durable because they contradict what the designer assumes:

High air flow before this. Pops at 216. Lower air flow by 260 to save energy. By 320 it's significantly less bitter.

Cracks 217–218 and 228–231. Nutty musk at 220. 233 green is coming out.

Roast 0.675 kg with 9mm/11mm post-dry, end bean temp 370.

These are Fahrenheit — consistent with Mode = 'F' and with the 31 roasts in the owner's log, which end between 341 °F and 362 °F. They are also bay nuts, not coffee.

The standalone designer opens every new profile at CHARGE 80, DRY_END 150, FC_START 190, DROP 210 — coffee landmarks, in Celsius. For this roastery every one of those numbers is wrong twice over. Two crack ranges (217–218 and 228–231) also suggest the single FC_START/FC_END pair does not describe this product well; that is worth a conversation before the defaults are simply swapped.

Note the batch size: 0.675 kg, against a UI currently reading 100 g. Whatever these fixes touch, do not assume the batch size shown is the batch size roasted.

Not a software problem

"The drum won't stay on less than 40 and stay on heat. Is this a mechanical issue?"

Cannot be answered from here, and it is not safe to guess: no write has ever been sent to this machine, so nothing in this repo has ever commanded the drum. Two possibilities worth separating at the roaster — a machine-side interlock that refuses low drum speed while the heater is on (which would be a safety feature, not a fault), or a genuine mechanical stall. Added to the Hardware pass.

Worth knowing: this bridge's own safety limits already assume the former. limits.json carries drum_min: 10 and refuses to drop the drum below it while the heater is on. If the machine's own floor is 40, that limit is set too low to be meaningful and should be raised to match reality once measured.
