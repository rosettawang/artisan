Listening for cracks, and using the machine's own state to know what to ignore

Record the roast, separate crack transients from drum and fan noise, and turn crack density into a number that can inform the drying phase. Artisan has no audio input of any kind today, so all of this is new. Low priority — filed to be ready, not to be built next.

The idea worth keeping

Most crack detectors are given a microphone and nothing else, so they spend their effort guessing what the machine sounds like. Artisan already knows. Heater, fan and drum percentages arrive on every sampling tick, straight from the Kaleido (HP, FC, RC).

So the noise model can be conditioned on machine state: fan at 60% has a known signature, and when the fan changes the expected noise changes with it — predictably, and a tick before the audio does. That converts the hard problem (blind source separation in a noisy room) into a much easier one (is this transient louder than what the fan at 60% and drum at 40% should sound like?).

This is the part of the design that is actually novel and the reason to build it here rather than use a generic tool.

Why cracks are separable at all

Machine noiseA crack
DurationContinuousImpulsive — milliseconds
SpectrumNarrow, tonal, stable (fan blade rate, drum rumble)Broadband click
Over timeStationary until an actuator changesSparse events, clustering into a rate

That difference is what onset detection exploits: track the spectral flux, subtract a running estimate of the stationary floor, and call the peaks. No machine learning is needed for a first version, and starting without it keeps the thing debuggable — a threshold you can see is worth more than a model you cannot.

The unhappy states, which is where the design is decided

Crack listener ▲ noise floor not learned

Counting is off. Run one empty preheat at the fan and drum settings you roast at, and the floor is learned in about a minute. Until then the roast is still recorded — nothing is lost, and it can be analysed afterwards.

Crack listener ▲ signal buried · 3 dB over floor

Counts below are unreliable and are shown greyed. Likely causes: fan above 80%, the mic has moved, or something else in the room is loud. The count is suppressed rather than guessed — a wrong crack count is worse than none, because it will be believed.

Crack listener ○ off

No microphone selected. Nothing is recorded and no audio leaves this machine.

Three rules the mockups are making concrete, and they matter more than the layout:

  • Recording and counting are separate. Audio is captured even when detection cannot run, because a recording can be re-analysed later and a missed roast cannot.
  • Suppress rather than guess. When the signal is buried, show no number. A plausible wrong count gets trusted and acted on.
  • Off means off. A microphone in a workplace needs an unambiguous off state, and it should be the default.

The claim this is meant to test

"You want to minimise crackling — too much moisture, so we extend the drying."

Mechanically plausible: crack vigour comes from steam pressure, so wetter material should crack harder. But it is a hypothesis about this product on this machine, and it has never been measured — which is precisely what makes it worth instrumenting.

Treated as a hypothesis throughout, and that is a methodological choice rather than a question for the owner. The consequence is concrete: nothing in this spec auto-extends drying. Phases A–C produce the measurement; only if C shows a correlation does D act on it, and even then advisory. If it turns out the claim is already settled fact for bay nuts, nothing here is wasted — the instrument still has to exist to use it.

The roast log is already half of the experiment. Humidity % is recorded for 15 of 31 roasts, ranging 10.1–12.7%, alongside soak/cure duration and drying method. What is missing is any measure of crackling. One note gets close — "too large a batch; popping at 216°F; foot-smelling from Cabello's dryer" — which shows the observation is already being made by hand, in prose, unquantified.

So Phase B's real output is not a feature; it is a column. Once crack density sits beside humidity for a dozen roasts, the hypothesis is either visible or it is not. Until then nothing should automatically extend anything.

Complication worth stating up front: the owner's own notes record two crack ranges — 217–218 °F and 228–231 °F. Coffee's first and second crack are far further apart and acoustically distinct. Whether these are two populations, two mechanisms, or one event with a gap is unknown, and a detector that assumes a single "first crack" will describe this product badly.

Microphone: dedicated, near the drum

Decided. Not the Mac's built-in: it sits metres away aimed at the room, and macOS applies automatic gain control, which pumps the noise floor up and down and defeats onset detection by construction — the very thing the whole design depends on. A cheap USB mic clamped near the drum head, with AGC off, is the requirement. The only owner-facing part is buying one, which is a purchase and not a design decision, so it is noted here rather than queued as a question.

Phases

A — Capture and calibrate

A new crack_listener.py. Pick a mic, record to a file beside the profile, and learn the noise floor from an empty preheat — sampled at several fan and drum settings, since those are the noise. No detection. Deliverable: a recording of a real roast and a noise fingerprint of the machine, which is what the rest needs to be developed against offline.

B — Detect and count

Spectral-flux onset detection against the state-conditioned floor. Emits crack events with timestamps, and writes crack density into the profile beside temperature. Tune it against the Phase A recordings, not live. The measure of success is agreement with a human listening to the same recording — so the first job is one hand-labelled roast to score against.

C — Show it

The strip and panel above, plus the counts in the roast record so they reach the log.

D — Only if C shows a correlation

Relate crack density to drying. Even then, advisory: surface "crack onset is 40 s earlier and denser than your reference — consider extending drying" and let the operator act. Nothing here should move a burner. If Phase C shows no correlation, D is not built, and the spec was still worth it — that is the answer arriving, not the project failing.

What is verified, and what is not

  • Verified Aug 6, 2026: Artisan has no audio input anywhere in artisanlib/ — no QAudioInput, QAudioSource, QMediaCaptureSession, pyaudio or sounddevice. This is entirely new surface, with no upstream code to conflict with. Also verified: the 15 humidity values and the "popping at 216°F" note, read from the roast log.
  • Not verified — and this is the risk that would kill it: that cracks are audible over this machine at all. Small batches (0.1–0.675 kg) make far fewer, quieter cracks than a commercial drum. Test this before writing any code — record one roast on a phone held near the drum and listen back. If a human cannot hear the cracks over the fan in that recording, no detector will, and the spec should be closed rather than built.
  • Unknown: bay nut crack acoustics. Everything published on roast crack detection is about coffee. Two crack ranges suggests this product does not behave like coffee acoustically either.
  • Not designed: retention and privacy. Continuous recording in a workplace needs a stated policy — how long files are kept, where they live, who can hear them. Decide before Phase A ships, not after.
